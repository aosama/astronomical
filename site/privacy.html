Privacy policy

Privacy Policy

Effective: November 2, 2026 · Applies to the Astronomical macOS application.

The short version

Astronomical runs language, vision, and image models entirely on your Mac. Your prompts, conversations, documents, and images are processed on your device and are never sent to us — we operate no servers that receive them. The app has no accounts, no sign-in, no advertising, and no in-app analytics. It sends a short problem report only when you choose to, and you see the whole report first.

What never leaves your Mac

  • Every inference request: prompts, model outputs, images, and documents you work with.
  • Your configuration, including the memory ceiling you set.
  • Prompt caches and model caches stored on your disk.

Network requests the app makes

The app makes a small number of network requests, none of which involve your content:

  • Model downloads. When you choose to install a model, the app downloads model files from public model repositories, such as Hugging Face. The repository sees a standard request for publicly published files. Downloading a model is always an action you initiate.
  • Update checks. Builds distributed outside the Mac App Store check a signed update feed to tell you when a new version exists. The feed operator sees standard web requests for the update description. App Store builds receive updates exclusively through the App Store and make no such request.
  • Skill updates. About once a day the app asks getastronomical.com whether a newer copy of its skill exists, the short guide that tells models on this Mac what Astronomical can do, and downloads it if so. The request carries nothing about you or your work, and a switch in Observatory's Settings turns it off.
  • Problem reports. Only when you choose to send one; see below.
  • Local API. The app serves a local interface on this Mac only, at a loopback address. Requests to it never leave your machine.

Coding agents you launch

astronomical launch starts a coding agent you installed yourself, such as Claude Code, Codex, or OpenCode, and points its model requests at the local API above, so the prompts and code it sends to the model are answered on this Mac. The agent remains its own program: anything it does over the network beyond those model requests, such as checking for its own updates or sending its maker's telemetry, is governed by that agent's settings and its maker's privacy policy, not by Astronomical. The launcher itself only asks the local app, over loopback, which models it serves; it sends nothing off this Mac and changes none of the agent's saved settings.

The OpenCode plugin

The Astronomical plugin for OpenCode, if you install it, asks the app on this Mac, over loopback, which models it serves, about every 30 seconds, and lists them in OpenCode. It sends nothing off this Mac. It writes one file, a log of what it did that never grows past 1 MB, in OpenCode's own state folder. What OpenCode itself does over the network is governed by OpenCode's settings and its maker's privacy policy, not by Astronomical.

Diagnostics and crash reports

The app contains no usage analytics. When something goes wrong, such as a model that will not load, a reply that runs out of memory, or a catalog download whose files do not match, the app can offer to send a short problem report.

  • You see the whole report first. The app shows every line that would be sent, and nothing is sent unless you press Send report. You can choose instead to send reports without being asked, or never to be offered one, under "What leaves this Mac" in Settings.
  • What a report holds. The app version; your Mac's chip, memory and macOS version; the name of the catalog model in use, or only the words "a model not in the catalog" for any other model; and the kind of problem. Nothing else: never your prompts, conversations, files, images, folder names or log lines.
  • Where it goes. To the developers' project at Sentry, an error-reporting service. Like any request over the internet, it reaches Sentry from your internet address. The project is set not to store internet addresses, and the app gives Sentry a placeholder address so no location is worked out from yours.
  • A record on your Mac. Every report sent is listed under "What leaves this Mac" in Settings, as the exact lines that went.
  • Mac App Store builds. If you have opted in (in System Settings) to sharing diagnostics and analytics with developers, Apple collects crash reports and aggregated usage statistics through Apple's own systems and shares them with us under Apple's privacy protections. We never receive your content, and individual identification is prevented by Apple's aggregation.
  • Direct-distribution builds. The system's own crash reports stay on your machine. They are sent nowhere unless you choose to attach one yourself when contacting support.

What we do not do

  • We do not collect, sell, or share personal data. There is no data to sell: no accounts exist.
  • We do not use advertising identifiers, device fingerprinting, or cross-app tracking.
  • We do not read files outside the locations the app uses for models, caches, logs, and your saved outputs.

Data stored on your Mac

The app stores downloaded model files, caches, logs, and your configuration on your disk in its designated application-support locations. You can remove everything by deleting the app's data locations or uninstalling the app; nothing is retained remotely.

Third-party components

Astronomical is open source. Direct-distribution builds embed the open-source Sparkle update framework, described above. App Store builds use Apple's built-in update mechanism. The full component list ships inside the application under Third-Party Notices.

Changes to this policy

If Astronomical's data practices ever change — for example, if an opt-in analytics feature ships — this page will be updated first, and the App Store privacy label will be updated in the same release.
